# Templates, Shells, and Sentence Banks

Reusable structures to start from. Adapt the content; do not leave a shell unfilled.

---

## 1. Topic ladder

```
Broad topic
  → Narrowed topic   (limit by population, place, time, or aspect)
  → Research question (one interrogative sentence)
  → Thesis           (one declarative sentence: position + reasons)
```

**Limiting operators** — apply two or three, never all:

| Operator | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Who exactly | *Chinese EFL undergraduates* |
| Setting | Where | *in a provincial university* |
| Time | When | *during the first year of study* |
| Aspect | Which slice of the topic | *grammatical accuracy, not overall quality* |
| Method | How it is examined | *in a quasi-experimental design* |
| Comparison | Against what | *compared with peer feedback* |

**Quality tests for a thesis**
- Could a reasonable person disagree? If no, it is a fact.
- Does it follow from a question? If not, it is a topic label.
- Does it preview the reasons? If not, the body has no map.
- Can it be supported in the allotted length? If not, narrow again.

## 2. Sentence outline shell

```
Thesis: [position], because [reason 1] and [reason 2].

I. Introduction
   A. Hook
   B. Background (2–4 sentences, no argument)
   C. Gap
   D. Thesis

II. Body 1 — [reason 1 as a complete claim]
   A. Topic sentence
   B. Evidence 1 + interpretation
   C. Evidence 2 + interpretation
   D. Closure and link

III. Body 2 — [reason 2 as a complete claim]
   (same internal structure)

IV. Counterargument — [strongest opposing claim]
   A. Statement of the opposing position, fairly
   B. Its strongest evidence
   C. Why it does not defeat the thesis (rebuttal)

V. Conclusion
   A. Reworded thesis
   B. Synthesis of the body's findings
   C. Implication, boundary, or open question

References
```

Topic outline (nouns only) is acceptable for a rough plan; the sentence outline is required for a draft, because a noun label hides whether you have a claim.

## 3. Abstract shell

```
[Background]      X has become central to Y, yet how Z operates remains unclear.
[Purpose]         This study examines whether A affects B among C.
[Method]          Data were drawn from N participants using M; analysis used T.
[Findings]        Results show that A produced a 23% improvement in B (p = .02), 
                  with no corresponding gain in C.
[Interpretation]  These findings suggest that A operates primarily on D.
[Boundary]        Because the sample was limited to C, generalization requires caution.
```

Keep every slot to one sentence. Delete the boundary sentence only if the journal or instructor forbids it.

## 4. Genre skeletons

**Argumentative essay**
```
Introduction (hook → background → claim → thesis)
Body 1: strongest supporting reason
Body 2: second reason
Body 3: counterargument and rebuttal
Conclusion (restate → implications → closing)
```

**Cause–effect essay**
```
Introduction with thesis naming the principal cause and its effect
Body 1: the cause, with mechanism
Body 2: the mediating conditions
Body 3: the effect, with evidence; distinguish correlation from causation
Conclusion: policy or research implication
```

**Compare–contrast essay**
```
Choose one pattern and hold it:
  Point-by-point: each body paragraph covers one criterion across both cases
  Block: one body per case, then a synthesis paragraph
Introduction states the criteria and the thesis (which case is preferable, or what the contrast reveals)
```

**Problem–solution essay**
```
Introduction: define the problem and why it resists current responses (thesis)
Body 1: causes or contributing conditions
Body 2: the proposed solution and its mechanism
Body 3: feasibility, cost, objections
Conclusion: what must happen first
```

**Literature review**
```
Introduction: scope, criteria for inclusion, the question organizing the review
Body: 3–4 thematic clusters, each ending in an evaluation
Synthesis paragraph: what the field agrees on, where it disagrees, what remains unexamined
Conclusion: the gap and its significance
```

## 5. Sentence bank

**Introducing the topic without cliché**
- *Over the past decade, X has moved from the margins of Y to the centre of policy debate.*
- *Few issues in Y have generated as much disagreement as X.*
- *The assumption that X is beneficial has become widespread; the evidence is less settled.*

Avoid opening with *With the development of society and economy* or *Since ancient times*. These are read as filler and mark the text immediately as translated prose.

**Reporting prior work**
- *A substantial literature examines X (Author, Year; Author, Year).*
- *Research on X has generally approached the problem from three directions: ... .*
- *The evidence on X is mixed: some studies report ... , whereas others find ... .*
- *Author (Year) offers the most systematic account to date, arguing that ... .*

**Establishing the gap**
- *These studies, however, share a common limitation: ... .*
- *Comparatively little attention has been paid to ... .*
- *Whether these findings hold for Y remains an open question.*
- *The mechanisms underlying this pattern have not been specified.*

**Stating purpose**
- *This study sets out to determine whether ... .*
- *The present paper examines how X is realized in Y, drawing on ... .*
- *Two questions guide the analysis: first, ... ; second, ... .*

**Integrating evidence**
- *Consistent with this account, Author (Year) finds that ... .*
- *This claim is supported by ... , although the sample was small.*
- *A different reading is possible: ... .*

**Interpretation (the sentence most often missing)**
- *This suggests that ... .*
- *One explanation is that ... .*
- *Taken together, these results indicate that ... .*
- *The pattern is unlikely to be explained by ... , since ... .*

**Concession and rebuttal**
- *Admittedly, ... . This objection, however, assumes that ... .*
- *While this criticism has force in the case of X, it does not apply to Y, because ... .*
- *The strength of this position lies in ... ; its weakness is ... .*

**Hedged conclusion**
- *The evidence presented here indicates that X is associated with Y, though the design cannot establish causation.*
- *These findings are consistent with the view that ... , but they do not exclude ... .*

**Signposting (use sparingly)**
- *Two findings deserve emphasis.*
- *The remainder of this section considers ... .* (one per paper at most)
- *This raises a further question: ... .*

## 6. Transition bank, by logical relation

| Relation | Connectives |
|---|---|
| Addition | moreover, furthermore, in addition, similarly |
| Contrast | however, nevertheless, by contrast, conversely, whereas |
| Concession | admittedly, although, while it is true that, even so |
| Cause | because, since, owing to, as a result of |
| Consequence | therefore, consequently, thus, accordingly, hence |
| Example | for instance, in particular, specifically, to illustrate |
| Sequence | first, second, subsequently, finally |
| Emphasis | indeed, in fact, more importantly |
| Restatement | in other words, that is, put differently |
| Summary | in sum, overall, taken together |

Rule: if deleting the connective leaves the logical relation intact, it was decoration. If deleting it destroys the relation, the sentences need restructuring, not a stronger connective.

## 7. Presentation shell (oral defence or class report)

**Timing**: 15 minutes → 12 minutes of content, 3 for questions. Three points maximum.

| Slide | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title, name, one-line framing of the question | No agenda slide |
| 2 | Why the question matters | One statistic or one concrete case |
| 3 | What is already known | Three findings, cited on screen |
| 4 | The gap | One sentence |
| 5 | Method | Diagram, not prose |
| 6–8 | Findings | One claim per slide; the slide title is the claim, e.g. *AI feedback cut errors by 23%* |
| 9 | Interpretation | Two sentences |
| 10 | Limitation and next step | One each |

**Delivery rules**
- Slide titles are sentences stating conclusions, not labels like *Results*.
- Maximum six lines per slide, font 24 pt or larger.
- Every figure carries a one-line takeaway in the caption.
- Rehearse to 80% of the time limit.
- In questions: if the question is unclear, ask back (*Do you mean X or Y?*). If the answer is outside the study, say so and name it as a next step rather than improvising a claim.
