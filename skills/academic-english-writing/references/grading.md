# Grading and Feedback

Reference for assessing a submitted paper, producing a defensible score, and turning the diagnosis into targeted advice plus a knowledge-point summary.

---

## 1. Purpose and boundary

Grading here means: read the paper against a fixed rubric, assign a score that can be traced to specific evidence in the text, then convert the defects into (a) concrete revision instructions and (b) a list of knowledge points the writer needs to repair.

Two boundaries that must hold:

- **Do not rewrite the paper.** Fix one representative sentence per defect type as a model. Rewriting the whole text destroys the diagnostic value and transfers authorship.
- **Do not inflate.** A generous score is a disservice: the writer will submit the same defects to a real grader. If the paper is weak, say so plainly and show why.

---

## 2. The 100-point rubric

| Dimension | Points | What is actually being judged |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis and content | 25 | Is the thesis contestable, specific, and sustained? Is the content on-topic and does it show independent thought? |
| Organization | 20 | Paragraph division, unity, coherence, transitions, introduction-to-conclusion arc |
| Evidence and reasoning | 20 | Relevance and sufficiency of evidence, interpretation of evidence, handling of counterargument, logical gaps |
| Language | 25 | Grammatical accuracy, sentence variety, academic register, lexical precision |
| Format and citation | 10 | Title, abstract, headings, reference list, in-text citations, word count, layout |

Sum to 100. Report the dimension scores, not only the total — the breakdown is what makes the feedback actionable.

### Band descriptors

| Dimension | Full marks (90%+) | Passing (60%) | Failing (<60%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis and content | Contestable claim, specific scope, every section serves it, some original angle | A claim exists but is vague, partly descriptive, or drifts mid-paper | No discernible claim; topic summary; or claim contradicted by the body |
| Organization | Clear paragraph functions, tight unity, transitions carry logic, conclusion answers the introduction | Paragraphs exist but some contain two ideas or none; transitions mechanical (*Firstly/Secondly*) | No paragraph logic; essay reads as a list of unrelated observations |
| Evidence and reasoning | Evidence is relevant, attributed, and interpreted; strongest counterargument addressed | Evidence present but partly generic or uninterpreted; counterargument absent or token | Assertion without support; or evidence pasted with no connection to the claim |
| Language | Accurate, varied, academic; errors do not distract | Meaning clear but with recurring article, tense, or agreement errors; register uneven | Errors impede comprehension; sentence structure breaks down |
| Format and citation | All conventions observed, citations and references correspond exactly | Minor inconsistencies; missing page numbers; reference list partly unordered | No reference list where required; uncited borrowed content |

### Deduction anchors (for consistency)

| Defect | Deduction | Dimension |
|---|---|---|
| No thesis, or thesis is a statement of fact / a question | −8 to −12 | Thesis |
| Thesis stated but abandoned after paragraph 2 | −5 | Thesis |
| Paragraph without a topic sentence carrying a claim | −2 each | Organization |
| Paragraph with two competing centres | −2 each | Organization |
| Evidence with no interpretation | −1 to −2 each | Evidence |
| Quotation used where paraphrase would do, or over 10% quoted | −2 | Evidence |
| Counterargument required by the genre but absent | −4 | Evidence |
| Article / agreement / tense error that obscures meaning | −1 each, cap −10 | Language |
| Comma splice or run-on | −0.5 each, cap −5 | Language |
| In-text citation without a matching reference entry (or the reverse) | −1 each | Format |
| Reference list absent where required | −5 | Format |
| Word count off by more than ±20% | −3 | Format |
| Undefined abbreviation, no title, single-spaced wall of text | −1 each | Format |

Calibration rule: read the whole paper once and form a global impression, then score dimension by dimension. If the total differs from the impression by more than 10 points, re-examine which dimension your impression was reacting to — usually language, which is the most visible and the least important.

## 3. Feedback output structure

Produce exactly this order. Do not lead with praise; lead with the judgement.

**① 一句话总评**
One sentence: the score band and the single dominant reason. Example: *结构完整、论点清楚，但全篇停在"陈述现象"，没有一处解释证据如何支持论点，因此落在 78 分档。*

**② 分数表**

| 维度 | 得分 | 满分 | 一句话依据 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 论点与内容 | 18 | 25 | thesis 可争议，但第三段起转向描述 |
| … | | | |

Total, band, and the one dimension that cost the most.

**③ 三处最值得改的地方**（按分数影响排序，不超过三处）
For each: 原文摘录 → 问题类型 → 为什么扣分 → 改写示范（只改这一句）→ 预期提分。

**④ 知识点归因**
Map each defect to a knowledge point using the table in §4. This is what turns a grade into learning.

**⑤ 下一步练习**
Two or three tasks, each under 20 minutes, each with a checkable completion criterion. Example: *把第三段拆成"主张—证据—解读"三句，改写后贴回来，我只看有没有那句解读。*

**⑥ 一句具体的肯定**
Name one thing the writer did that most classmates do not, located by paragraph. Not *写得不错*, but *第二段先承认 AI 的效率优势再转折，这个让步让论证稳了很多*.

## 4. Error-to-knowledge-point map

| Observed defect | Underlying knowledge point | Where it is taught |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis is a fact or a question | Contestability test: could a reasonable person disagree? | `structure.md` §3 · `templates.md` §1 |
| Thesis with no reason preview | Thesis as position + reasons | `templates.md` §1 |
| Introduction spends three paragraphs on background | Funnel proportion; the gap must arrive early | `structure.md` §3 |
| Body paragraphs read as a list of topics | Topic sentence must carry a claim | `structure.md` §10 · `editing.md` §3 |
| Evidence followed by nothing | PIE: the Explanation step | `structure.md` §10 |
| Citation at paragraph end only | Attribution granularity | `citations.md` §1 |
| Paraphrase mirrors the source | Structural paraphrase vs patchwriting | `citations.md` §4 |
| No counterargument | Argumentative genre requirements | `templates.md` §4 |
| Tense jumps inside the results paragraph | Section-based tense rules | `language.md` §5 |
| Heavy *very / a lot of / things* | Register and precision | `language.md` §2 |
| Claims stronger than evidence | Hedging calibration | `language.md` §3 |
| Missing articles, comma splices | L1 interference inventory | `language.md` §7 |
| References unordered or inconsistent | Reference list mechanics | `citations.md` §2 |
| Conclusion introduces a new idea | Function of the conclusion | `structure.md` §8 |

Report the top three knowledge points only. A list of fourteen defects is not feedback; it is a verdict.

## 5. Grading integrity rules

1. **Every deduction must be locatable.** Quote the sentence or name the paragraph. A score without a location cannot be disputed and cannot be learned from.
2. **Do not average away a fatal defect.** A paper with excellent language and no argument is not a good paper. Weight the argument dimensions before the language dimension when the genre is argumentative.
3. **Do not grade the topic.** Judge the execution, not whether you find the subject interesting.
4. **Do not reward length.** More words with the same content is not more work.
5. **Distinguish error from choice.** A deliberate sentence fragment in a conclusion may be effective; the same fragment in a methods section is an error. Ask whether the choice works before deducting.
6. **State what you could not judge.** If the assignment brief is unknown, say which criteria you assumed.
7. **Never supply a full rewritten version** in grading mode. Supply one sentence per defect type.

---

## 6. Fast triage (when the user wants a verdict in one message)

Run these six checks in order and stop reporting at the first failure that is severe enough to cap the score:

1. Is there a contestable thesis? If no — the paper cannot exceed 70 regardless of polish.
2. Does every paragraph serve it? If no — cap at 80.
3. Is every claim evidenced and every piece of evidence interpreted? If no — cap at 85.
4. Is the citation apparatus consistent? If no — lose Format points, do not cap.
5. Are there comprehension-blocking errors? If yes — cap at 75.
6. Is there anything original — a non-obvious comparison, a genuine counterargument, a specific example? If yes, allow the 90+ band.

This triage is for a quick read. For a submission that will be contested, use the full rubric.
