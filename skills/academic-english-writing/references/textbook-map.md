# Textbook Map — Writing Research Papers

Mapping between the course textbook and the operational references in this skill.

**Textbook**: 《学术英语论文写作》 (Writing Research Papers), Dorothy E. Zemach, Daniel Broudy, Chris Valvona, with 高霄; FLTRP 2015, imported from Macmillan Writing Series level 4. ISBN 978-7-5135-6187-7. 152 pages, 12 units.

**Approach of the book**: process writing (prewriting → outlining → drafting → revising) plus peer review, with APA documentation. Each unit pairs explanation with controlled practice and a *Put it together* task. The book is deliberately short; it teaches the skeleton of the process, not exhaustive coverage.

## Known gaps in the textbook, covered here

1. **APA edition drift.** The 2015 imported edition follows APA conventions current at the time. APA 7th edition changed several rules (e.g. up to 20 authors listed, *Retrieved from* removed, DOIs as full links, singular *they*). `citations.md` follows APA 7.
2. **Chinese-L1 interference.** The book is written for an international market and does not address article use, comma splices, *there be* overuse, or connective confusion. `language.md` §7 fills this.
3. **Empirical sections.** The book's model is an essay-derived research paper; IMRAD sections (methods, results, discussion) receive limited treatment. `structure.md` §5–§8 supplies the moves, statistics reporting conventions, and section-level errors.
4. **Rubric alignment.** Chinese university EAP courses typically grade on thesis clarity, organization, evidence use, language accuracy, and citation format. `editing.md` §4 is written to that rubric.

## Suggested study sequence

Use this when the user asks how to work through the book rather than how to write a specific paper.

| Stage | Units | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Diagnostic | 1 | Rewrite one old essay's thesis; mark every sentence that does not serve it |
| 2. Prewriting | 2, 4 | One research question plus twelve note cards with page numbers and type tags |
| 3. Planning | 5 | One sentence outline, Roman-numeral lines readable as an argument |
| 4. Integrity | 6, 9 | Paraphrase three passages; label each as quotation, paraphrase, or summary with justification |
| 5. Drafting | 7, 8, 10 | Full first draft; no editing during drafting; tense audit afterwards |
| 6. Revising | 11, 3 | Three-pass revision plus one peer exchange using the appendix forms |
| 7. Delivery | 12 | Ten-slide report with claim-style slide titles |

Each stage should produce an artifact. Reading the units without producing the artifact leaves the skill unlearned — the book's own design assumes the exercises are done.
